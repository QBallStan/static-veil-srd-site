Nobody in 2092 gets far alone. The gear comes from a fixer, the bullet comes out at a street doc's, the name on the file comes from a cop who owes you. A **Contact** is someone in your network who can do what you can't, at a price.

> **Mama Ruiz:** Fixer · Connection 4 · Competence 5 · Favors +1

- **Field:** what they do and where their reach lies (fixer, street doc, armorer, talismonger, cop, journalist…). It sets which requests they're competent for.
- **Connection (1–6):** how big and how well placed their network is.
- **Competence (1–8):** their skill in their Field.
- **Favors (−3 to +5):** how much they owe you. Below 0, you owe them.

## Asking for Help

When a Contact does something in their Field, such as finding gear, digging up information or making an introduction, **they roll**, with a Success Target of **Competence × 10** and **Connection** [[Bonus Dice]]. Sometimes it makes more sense for you to do the legwork with their help, such as following up a lead they gave you or using their name at the door. Then you roll, and add their Connection dice. Outside their Field, they're an ordinary person.

Sourcing through a Contact works the same way: they make the [[Availability and Markets#Sourcing|Sourcing Test]].

## Favors

What a request costs depends on how big it is:

| Request | Cost | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Business** | The usual price | Standard gear, a known piece of information, a routine introduction |
| **A favor** | 1 Favor | Something illegal, urgent, risky for them, or outside their habits |
| **A big favor** | 2 or more | Hiding the crew, putting their business on the line, burning one of their own contacts |

A Contact at 0 or below still helps, and the cost pushes the balance further down. At **−3**, they won't work for you again until you've paid off your debt.

**When you owe them,** they call it in sooner or later: a job, a package, keeping quiet. Turning them down costs Favors, or the Contact.

**Earning Favors:** a job done for them, a real service, protection, a big tip or a good word: +1, up to 5. Betraying them, endangering them or leading trouble to their door costs Favors, or the Contact entirely.
