**Actions** are optional custom rules that make an NPC or creature easier to run, or give it a signature move. A profile doesn't need any if its Skills, weapons, gear, spells and [[Creature Traits|Traits]] already cover everything.

Each Action gives its [[Initiative and Action Points#Action Points and Actions|category and AP cost]], even when that's just the usual cost for a Free, Minor or Major Action. Then it gives whatever else it needs: what it does, the weapon or test, how many targets, range or area, any movement that's part of it, damage and side effects, and any special rule for defending against it.

An Action can say who's eligible as a target: one target within Reach, everyone in an area, whoever the creature is grappling. Which eligible target it goes for is always the GM's choice.

Actions don't stop a character from using the normal rules, unless the profile says so. An Action that makes several attacks is an exception to the [[The Combat Exchange#One Attack per Turn|one attack per turn]] rule. Resolve each attack normally, unless the Action says otherwise.

## Action Lists

The simplest way is a list: when the character acts, the GM picks whichever Action fits. That's how NPCs who think normally use them.

## Action Tables

When chance makes a character easier or more interesting to run, number its Actions in a table. When it's about to act, roll the die shown before spending AP, then pay the Action's cost and resolve it.

The GM can always pick instead of rolling. If the result has no valid target, costs more AP than the character has left, or doesn't make sense, reroll or choose something else. Putting an Action in several entries makes it more likely, with no extra rule needed.

Action Tables suit instinct-driven creatures, automated systems, drones and entities with strange or rigid behavior. They're never required.
