
## Initiative

When combat begins, each participant rolls Initiative:

**1d10 + [[Sub-Attributes#Initiative Bonus|Initiative Bonus]]**

The highest result acts first, and turns go down from there. The order stays the same for the whole fight unless a rule changes it, and it's used again for every pass. A combatant who holds AP for later doesn't lose their place.

If two or more combatants tie, player characters go before NPCs. Tied players decide their order among themselves, and the GM decides the order of tied NPCs.

A player character can spend [[Karma]] to act first in a round. See [[Karma#Jump the Line|Jump the Line]].

### Group Initiative

When several combatants act as a unit, the GM can make one Initiative roll for the whole group. When that Initiative comes up, every member acts, in any order they like. Each member still tracks their own AP.

Group Initiative works well for squads, groups of minor NPCs, [[Chases and Vehicle Combat#Setting Up|vehicle crews]], drones under the same controller and other coordinated groups.

### Delaying and Holding Actions

On your turn, you can **Delay** to act later in the Initiative order. Pick a combatant who hasn't acted yet in this pass, or choose to go at the end of the pass, and take your turn at that point. Your new position becomes your Initiative for the rest of the fight. You can't Delay into a later pass. If you choose not to act when your delayed turn comes up, you lose 1 AP as normal.

You can also **Hold an Action** by naming a specific action and a specific trigger, such as firing when an enemy comes through a doorway. Spend the action's AP straight away. If the trigger happens before your next turn, the held action interrupts it: it resolves right after the trigger, before the triggering character does anything else. If the trigger doesn't happen before your next turn, the action is lost and the AP stays spent.

A held action has to be specific enough for the GM to tell when it triggers. If the action or trigger is too vague, the GM can ask you to narrow it down first.

## Action Points and Actions

Every combatant has a pool of **Action Points (AP)** for each round. Characters have 3 AP by default. Spells, cyberware, Traits and other effects can raise or lower that. AP refills at the start of every round.

On their turn, a combatant can spend AP in any order, up to **4 AP per turn**. They don't have to spend it all, and can keep the rest for later passes or for [[Interrupt Actions]]. AP spent on Interrupt Actions outside their turn doesn't count toward the 4 AP limit. Moving is covered in [[Movement and Positioning]].

### Free Actions

A **Free Action** costs nothing. It's anything that takes almost no time or attention, such as shouting a warning, dropping something, making a gesture or saying a few words. There's no fixed limit, but the GM can rein in anything excessive.

### Minor Actions

A **Minor Action** costs 1 AP. It's something quick or supporting, such as drawing a weapon, reloading, opening a door, using a device within reach or triggering a minor ability.

### Major Actions

A **Major Action** costs 2 AP or more. Attacks are Major Actions, as is anything else that takes real effort or focus, such as hacking under pressure, casting most spells, giving emergency treatment or forcing open a barrier. Major Actions that cost more than 2 AP say so in their rules.
