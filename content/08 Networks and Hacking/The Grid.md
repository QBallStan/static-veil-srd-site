**The Grid**, formally the **Global Routing Interface Design (GRID)**, is the infrastructure that carries nearly all data: communication, identity, payments, AR, devices, services, transport, security and corporate networks. Before the 2075 Blackout, it was called **the Net**. The Grid is what was built in its place: Shards, Arrays and Matrices, hardened systems and stricter physical control, not a simple restoration of the old seamless network.

It isn't a separate virtual world. It runs through ordinary life. Doors check credentials through it, cars trade traffic data, payments and IDs are confirmed through it, and plenty of equipment stops working properly without it. Most people use it through a [[Interfaces#PADs|PAD]], [[Interfaces#Augmented Reality|AR]] and implants. [[Hacking|GridRunners]] use [[Interfaces#Decks and Cold Cradles|Decks]] to make it do things it shouldn't.

## Devices and Connectivity

Most modern devices stay connected all the time. A device normally has to be connected to be hacked remotely.

A character can disconnect one of their devices as a [[Initiative and Action Points#Free Actions|Free Action]]. While it's disconnected:

- It can't normally be hacked remotely.
- If it relies on its connection to work fully, tests that use it take −20 (more than a [[Device Damage|Glitched]] device's −10).
- Anything that depends on a Grid connection stops working, and some devices shut down entirely, as their rules say.

Reconnecting takes **one round**. A direct physical connection can still reach a disconnected device, but it doesn't get past the device's security.

## Hosts, Personal Networks and Devices

These are what GridRunners hack.

### Hosts

**Hosts** are managed systems: a bank portal, a hospital's records, a corporate office, a building's security grid. Most devices are slaved to one, so hacking a building's doors means hacking its Host. A facility can run several Hosts, and getting into one doesn't get you into the others. Each Host has a [[Hacking#Firewall|Firewall]] rating that sets how hard it is to break into and how quickly it notices intruders, and runs its own IC (see [[Hacking|Hacking]]).

### Personal Networks

A **personal network** is a character and their connected gear: PAD, weapons, augmentations, sensors. It's one target. Getting in reaches everything connected to it, at the normal [[Hacking#Authority|authority]] difficulty for whatever you're trying to do. A device that's been disconnected from the network can't be reached through it.

### Standalone Devices

A **standalone device** runs on its own, with an onboard defensive suite and its own Firewall. Drones on independent patrol, isolated terminals and some military gear work this way.

## Grid Architecture

The rest of the Grid is architecture: the route to a target, not a target itself.

### Shards

**Shards** are local network environments formed by the devices in an area. They have no fixed size. In a packed district, a Shard might cover about 100 m². In empty countryside, one might stretch for several kilometers. Shards split, merge and shift as conditions change.

Shards exist to compartmentalize traffic. Each Shard between the GridRunner's own and the target's gives **−10** to hacking tests against that target. The GM decides how many Shards lie in between. That makes hacking a Host on the other side of a city extremely hard, and reaching one in another country or on another continent next to impossible without exceptional means.

A [[Interfaces#Hardlines|Hardline]] to the target ignores Shard distance and gives +10.

### Arrays and Matrices

**Arrays** group Shards into managed territories, such as a city, an enclave, a transit network or a campus. **Matrices** link Arrays at continental, national or corporate scale, carrying the Grid's backbone, identity and routing. Neither is hacked. Their boundaries mostly decide which authority, including which branch of GridWatch, would investigate an intrusion afterwards.

### The Nexus

**The Nexus** is a huge public virtual city running on a major Host: a place to meet, trade, advertise, work and use the services that keep a presence there. It isn't a map of the Grid. A corporation's tower in the Nexus is only the service they chose to show. It doesn't lead into their other systems, and it doesn't bypass Shard distance.
