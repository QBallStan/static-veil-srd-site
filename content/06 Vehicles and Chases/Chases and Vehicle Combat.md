When a chase starts, the vehicles stop being transportation. Corporate sedans tear through rain-slick streets, courier bikes split lanes under the holo-signs, and an escape route turns into a running gunfight. Chases use the normal combat rules: [[Combat Basics#Rounds, Passes, and Turns|rounds]], [[Initiative and Action Points#Initiative|Initiative]], [[Initiative and Action Points#Action Points and Actions|AP]] and attacks. The drivers just have a few extra things they can do.

## Setting Up

Decide what each side wants: to escape, to catch the other, to board, to reach somewhere, or to destroy a target. Then note each vehicle's **Current Speed** in [[Operating Vehicles#Speed Bands|Speed Bands]] and the **gap** between vehicles, in meters.

Each vehicle and everyone aboard act on the driver's Initiative, in any order. Each character keeps their own AP.

## The Gap

At the start of each round, compare the vehicles' Current Speeds. For each Speed Band of difference, the gap changes by:

| Environment | Per Speed Band |
|---|---:|
| Tight: city streets, alleys, rivers, traffic | 25 m |
| Open: highways, open ground, open water, the air | 100 m |

The faster vehicle closes or opens the gap, depending on who's chasing whom. Use the gap as the range for every weapon, with each weapon's own [[Ranged Combat#Range Bands|Range Bands]].

The quarry escapes once it's beyond Extreme range of every pursuer's weapons and out of sight, or when the GM rules that the pursuers have lost it.

## Speed

Once per round, regardless of how many turns they take, the driver can change Current Speed as a Free Action: up by as many bands as the vehicle's [[Operating Vehicles#Speed Bands|Acceleration]], or down by up to 2 bands, without going over the vehicle's Speed. Braking harder needs a Stunt.

## Driver Actions

Most driving needs no roll: following a road, avoiding obvious obstacles, keeping pace. When the driver wants to do something more, they make a [[Operating Vehicles#Vehicle Tests|Vehicle Test]] as a Major Action (2 AP), adding the vehicle's [[Operating Vehicles#Handling|Handling dice]]:

- **Evade:** drive erratically. Each [[Success Targets and Hits#Hits|Hit]] gives −5 to attacks against the vehicle and its occupants, *and* to attacks made from it, until the driver's next turn.
- **Weave:** in a tight environment, push through obstacles at speed. Choose a Weave score from 1 up to your Current Speed, and take −10 per point. On a failure, you crash at your Current Speed. On a success, each pursuer must Weave at the same score on their next turn, with the same risk of crashing. A pursuer who doesn't falls back: the gap grows by one gap step per point of Weave.
- **Ram:** drive into another vehicle within about 10 meters. The target's Evade penalty applies. On a success, both vehicles collide. Use the difference in their Speeds, or the total if they hit head-on, or the rammer's full Speed against something standing still.
- **Stunt:** anything else the vehicle could physically do: cut through a mall, jump a gap, pull up alongside so someone can board, brake hard, or line up a shot for a gunner. The GM sets the difficulty.
- **Dogfight:** see below.

A vehicle with no one at the controls becomes [[Operating Vehicles#Uncontrolled Vehicles|Uncontrolled]].

## Dogfighting

When aircraft, or other vehicles that can move freely around each other, are within about 1 km and within 1 Speed Band of each other, their pilots can fight for position. The Dogfight is the pilot's Vehicle Test for the round, [[Opposed Tests|opposed]] against each hostile pilot engaging them. A pilot takes −10 for each hostile vehicle engaging them beyond the first.

The winning pilot chooses which of their own fire arcs the opponent is in, and which of the opponent's arcs they're in. They get +10 to attacks against that opponent this round, and the opponent gets −10 to attacks against them. On a tie, neither can use fixed weapons against the other. Turrets still fire normally.

A pilot who wins against every vehicle engaging them can **break off** and leave the dogfight. If the others follow, it becomes a chase.

## Shooting

Attacks to or from a moving vehicle take **−5 per Speed Band of difference** between attacker and target, plus any Evade penalty.

Mounted weapons can only fire into their arc. A **fixed** weapon fires to the front or rear, a **pintle** mount covers a limited arc, and a **turret** can fire in any direction. A target ahead of you is in your front arc, and one behind you is in your rear arc.

Occupants can be targeted directly when they're visible. The vehicle's doors, panels and armor give them [[Cover and Shields#Cover|cover]], depending on the design and where the shot comes from. An occupant fully hidden by the bodywork can only be hit through it (see [[Objects, Sneak Attacks, and Surprise#Attacking Through a Barrier|Attacking Through a Barrier]]). An exposed rider gets no cover.

## Collisions

A collision deals **2 × Speed flat damage, plus Speed d6 as [[Bonus Dice]]**. With no test, they always count. In a head-on collision, add both vehicles' Speeds together. If whatever was hit is solid, both sides take the damage.

Occupants take the same damage. [[Sub-Attributes#Grit|Grit]] applies, but personal Armor doesn't. Belted occupants take half, rounded down. Unbelted riders of open vehicles, such as motorcycles, are also thrown 5 meters per Speed Band.
