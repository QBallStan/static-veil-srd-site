Most vehicles can be operated without a test. Driving through traffic, following a route, docking in calm water or holding a steady flight doesn't need a roll when nothing is at stake. Roll when speed, terrain, weather, damage, hostile action or a difficult maneuver makes control uncertain and failure would matter. When a pursuit or a fight breaks out, use [[Chases and Vehicle Combat]].

## Operating Skills

- **Driving** (Core Skill): cars, motorcycles, trucks, tracked vehicles and other ordinary ground vehicles.
- **Boating** (Expertise): watercraft, from surface vessels to submersibles.
- **Piloting** (Expertise): aircraft, VTOLs, walkers and anything else with specialized controls.

A character can try a closely related vehicle at the GM's discretion, usually with a penalty. Some highly specialized vehicles can't be operated at all without the right Expertise.

## Vehicle Tests

A **Vehicle Test** normally uses **Instinct + Driving, Boating or Piloting** (see [[Skills]]). Agility can replace Instinct when the situation is more about precise control than reaction. Each Vehicle Test resolves one maneuver, one hazard, or one stretch of difficult driving.

## Handling

**Handling** (1 to 5) is how responsive and forgiving the vehicle is. When a test is about steering, maneuvering or staying in control, add one [[Bonus Dice|Bonus Die]] per point of Handling. Handling doesn't help with navigation, repairs, mounted weapons or anything else that isn't controlling the vehicle.

## Speed Bands

Vehicle speeds use **Speed Bands**. A vehicle's **Speed** is the highest band it can normally reach, and its **Current Speed** is the band it's moving at right now.

| Band | Rating | Speed |
|---|--:|--:|
| Stopped | 0 | 0 km/h |
| Idle | 1 | 1–20 km/h |
| Very Slow | 2 | 21–50 km/h |
| Slow | 3 | 51–100 km/h |
| Medium | 4 | 101–200 km/h |
| High | 5 | 201–300 km/h |
| Fast | 6 | 301–500 km/h |
| Very Fast | 7 | 501–800 km/h |
| Subsonic | 8 | 801–1,200 km/h |

The speeds are benchmarks, not hard limits.

A vehicle's **Acceleration** is how many Speed Bands it can gain in one round. In a chase, Current Speed changes once per round (see [[Chases and Vehicle Combat#Speed|Speed]]).

| Acceleration | Examples |
|---|---|
| 1 | Trucks, buses, boats, heavy armor, walkers |
| 2 | Ordinary cars, vans, most aircraft |
| 3 | Sports cars, racing bikes, interceptors |

## Terrain and Conditions

The GM applies [[Modifiers|modifiers]] when terrain, weather, visibility or traffic makes a vehicle harder to control: mud, ice, dense traffic, rough water, turbulence, poor visibility. What matters depends on the vehicle too. A tracked vehicle shrugs off broken ground that would stop a low-slung sports car. Vehicle Traits can reduce, ignore or worsen specific penalties. Only stack penalties that represent separate problems.

## Crews and Passengers

A vehicle's **Crew** is the minimum number of people needed to run it normally. With fewer, the crew may take penalties or be unable to use some systems. Each crew member can only use the controls at their own station, such as driver, gunner, navigator or engineer. Moving between stations in a moving vehicle can take an action, or a test if space is tight or the ride is rough. Passengers aren't crew unless they take a station. Exposed passengers can be targeted normally. Enclosed ones get [[Cover and Shields#Cover|cover]] from the bodywork.

## Uncontrolled Vehicles

A moving vehicle becomes **Uncontrolled** when no one conscious is at the controls, the operator can't act, or a rule says control is lost.

An Uncontrolled ground vehicle or boat keeps going in a straight line and loses 1 Speed Band at the end of each round until it stops. Terrain, currents, obstacles and damage can change its course or stop it much sooner.

An Uncontrolled aircraft comes down, depending on its condition:

- **Gliding** (fixed wings still working): loses 1 Speed Band per round, down to a minimum of 2.
- **Autorotating** (a rotorcraft with an intact rotor): settles at Speed 2–3.
- **Falling** (anything else): speeds up 1 Speed Band per round, up to 5, or 6 for something streamlined or very dense.

Anyone who can reach the controls can try a Vehicle Test to regain control. The difficulty depends on the vehicle's speed, condition, altitude and surroundings.
