A **Critical Wound** is catastrophic trauma that takes a character out of the fight and fills a Critical Wound Slot. A **Critical Injury** is the specific damage it leaves behind.

When lethal damage drops a character to 0 [[Wounds|Health]], the attack's [[Hit Locations and Damage#Hit Locations|Hit Location]] decides which table to roll on. Psychic damage, Hot Dive [[Interfaces#Snapback|Snapback]] and [[Spellcasting#Strain|Strain]] have their own tables. Roll 1d100, with these modifiers:

- **+5 for each point of damage past 0 Health.**
- **+10 for each Critical Injury the character already has.**

The modifiers stack. Anything above 100 counts as 100. For example, a character at 3 Health takes 7 Final Damage. The first 3 points take them to 0, and the other 4 add +20 to the roll.

Apply the result from the right table. An injury to an arm or leg affects the limb that was hit. Its effects come on top of being [[Death and Incapacitation|Incapacitated]].

## Injury Terms

These terms mean the same thing in every table.

### Unstable

The injury is immediately life-threatening. Each Unstable injury lists how long the character has, such as **Unstable (1d3 rounds)** or **Unstable (1d6 rounds)**. When that time runs out, the character dies unless they've been [[Healing and Medical Care#Stabilization|stabilized]].

Stabilization stops them dying but doesn't remove the injury, start its recovery or count as any other treatment it needs. If an injury needs [[Healing and Medical Care#Advanced Treatment|Advanced]] or [[Healing and Medical Care#Extraordinary Treatment|Extraordinary Treatment]] to survive, stabilizing only buys time: that treatment still has to happen before the new deadline runs out.

### Treatment Required

The injury doesn't start recovering until it's [[Healing and Medical Care#Treating Critical Injuries|properly treated]]: setting a bone, closing a major wound, removing something lodged in the body, fixing a dislocation, or whatever the injury needs. Stabilization doesn't count unless it also deals with the injury itself.

### Advanced Treatment

The injury needs surgery or another specialized procedure. Basic treatment can stabilize the patient and meet ordinary treatment needs, but it can't prevent a Permanent consequence that calls for Advanced Treatment. Advanced Treatment has to be done before the injury finishes recovering.

### Extraordinary Treatment

The injury is beyond ordinary medicine. It may need experimental surgery, organ replacement, neurological repair or major reconstruction, with the specialists, equipment and facilities to match. It can't normally be attempted without the right medical Expertise.

### Permanent

The consequence stays after the injury has otherwise healed: a lost limb, a destroyed sense, paralysis, a reduced Attribute. If the injury says treatment can prevent it, that treatment has to be done before recovery ends. After that, only treatment, augmentation, magic or another effect that restores the lost function can remove it.

### Permanent Attribute Reductions

The character's natural Attribute drops by the listed amount, to a minimum of 0. Recalculate any [[Sub-Attributes]] that use it straight away. The Attribute can be raised again through normal advancement, which represents rehabilitation and retraining. This doesn't restore a missing limb, a lost sense or any other separate consequence. If treatment can prevent the reduction, it has to be done before recovery ends. Otherwise, the reduction takes effect when recovery ends.

### Attributes Reduced to 0

If any of a character's natural [[Attributes]] is permanently reduced to 0, they can no longer be played. Depending on the Attribute and the injury, they may die, fall into a coma, lose all physical function or lose meaningful awareness. Temporary bonuses, equipment, augmentation and magic don't prevent this. The natural Attribute has to be restored above 0 first.

## Critical Injury Recovery

Recovery times assume ordinary 2092 clinical care: modern drugs, surgical support and supervised rehabilitation. Poor conditions or bad care can make recovery take longer, at the GM's discretion.

Once any required treatment is done, roll the injury's Recovery time. The injury stays active until that time has passed.

Rest and care can speed things up. Once a week, if the character has spent at least 4 days resting, being cared for or doing only light activity, they make a [[Skills#Attribute Checks|Vigor check]]. Each [[Success Targets and Hits#Hits|Hit]] takes 1 extra day off the remaining Recovery time, up to 7 days per check. A failure gives no extra recovery, but time still passes as normal. A character with several Critical Injuries makes one check per week and splits the days however they like. A [[Complications|Complication]] adds 1d6 days to one active Critical Injury, after the days from the check are taken off.

Strenuous activity, new injuries, poor living conditions or a lack of care can rule out the weekly check, at the GM's discretion.
