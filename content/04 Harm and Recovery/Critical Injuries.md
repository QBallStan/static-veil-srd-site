A **Critical Wound** represents catastrophic trauma that incapacitates an Operator and fills a Critical Wound Slot. A **Critical Injury** describes the specific lasting consequence of that trauma.

When lethal damage reduces an Operator to **0 Health**, use the attack's [[Hit Locations and Damage#Hit Locations|Hit Location]] to determine which Critical Injury table applies. Psychic Critical Wounds ignore Hit Location and use the [[Critical Injuries#Psychic Critical Injuries|Psychic Critical Injuries]] table instead.

Roll 1d100 and apply the following modifiers:

- **+5 for each point of damage that would reduce Health below 0.**
- **+10 for each active Critical Injury.**

These modifiers stack. A modified result above 100 counts as 100.

For example, an Operator at 3 Health suffers 7 Final Damage. The first 3 damage reduces them to 0 Health, while the remaining 4 damage exceeds 0. The Critical Injury roll therefore gains +20.

Consult the appropriate Critical Injury table and apply the result for the affected Hit Location. If an arm or leg was struck, the injury affects that specific limb.

Critical Injury effects apply in addition to [[Death and Incapacitation|Incapacitation]] caused by the Critical Wound.

## Injury Terms

Critical Injury entries use a small set of terms to describe immediate danger, treatment requirements, recovery, and lasting consequences.

These terms have the same meaning wherever they appear in the Critical Injury tables.

### Unstable

An **Unstable** injury is immediately life-threatening.

The injury states how long the character has before death, such as 1d6 rounds, 1d6 minutes, or 1d6 hours.

When this time expires, the character dies unless the injury has been stabilized.

[[Healing and Medical Care#Stabilization|Stabilization]] prevents immediate death but does not remove the Critical Injury, begin recovery, or satisfy any additional treatment requirements.

If an injury states that survival requires [[Healing and Medical Care#Advanced Treatment|Advanced Treatment]] or [[Healing and Medical Care#Extraordinary Treatment|Extraordinary Treatment]], stabilization only delays death. The required treatment must still be completed before the new deadline expires.

### Treatment Required

An injury marked **[[Healing and Medical Care#Treating Critical Injuries|Treatment Required]]** does not begin recovering until it has been properly treated.

Treatment may involve setting and immobilizing a broken bone, closing a major wound, safely removing a lodged object, cleaning damaged tissue, correcting a dislocation, or another procedure appropriate to the injury.

Stabilization does not satisfy Treatment Required unless the care provided also addresses the injury itself.

### Advanced Treatment

An injury that refers to **Advanced Treatment** requires surgery or another specialized medical procedure.

Basic treatment may stabilize the patient and satisfy ordinary treatment requirements, but it cannot prevent a permanent consequence that specifically requires Advanced Treatment.

Advanced Treatment must be completed successfully before the injury finishes recovering.

### Extraordinary Treatment

An injury that requires **Extraordinary Treatment** cannot be adequately treated with ordinary medical resources.

Extraordinary Treatment may require experimental surgery, organ replacement, extensive neurological repair, major tissue reconstruction, or another exceptional procedure appropriate to the injury.

The necessary specialist, equipment, facility, and technology depend on the treatment required.

Extraordinary Treatment cannot normally be attempted without the appropriate specialized medical Expertise.

### Permanent

Some Critical Injuries list a **Permanent** consequence.

Permanent consequences remain after the injury has otherwise recovered. They may include lost limbs, destroyed senses, permanent Attribute reductions, paralysis, or other lasting impairments.

If the injury states that a Permanent consequence can be prevented by Advanced Treatment or Extraordinary Treatment, that treatment must be completed successfully before the injury finishes recovering.

Permanent consequences can only be removed later by treatment, augmentation, magic, or another effect capable of restoring the lost function.

### Permanent Attribute Reductions

A permanent Attribute reduction lowers the character's natural Attribute by the listed amount.

An Attribute cannot be reduced below 0.

Recalculate any [[Sub-Attributes]] or other values derived from the reduced Attribute immediately.

A permanently reduced Attribute may later be increased through normal advancement. This represents adaptation, rehabilitation, or retraining, but does not restore a missing limb, destroyed sense, paralysis, or another separate consequence of the injury.

If an injury states that Advanced Treatment or Extraordinary Treatment can prevent an Attribute reduction, that treatment must be completed successfully before the injury finishes recovering. Otherwise, the reduction takes effect when recovery ends.

### Attributes Reduced to 0

If a character's natural Strength, Agility, Fortitude, Logic, Resolve, Charisma, Instinct, or [[Attributes#Anima|Anima]] is permanently reduced to 0, they are no longer viable as an Operator.

Depending on the Attribute, injury, and circumstances, the character may die, fall into a coma, suffer total physical incapacity, lose meaningful awareness, or otherwise become unable to participate in play.

Temporary bonuses, equipment, augmentation, magic, and situational modifiers do not prevent this consequence. The character's natural Attribute must be restored above 0 before they can become playable again.

## Critical Injury Recovery

Once any required treatment has been completed, roll the Recovery duration listed by the Critical Injury. The injury remains active until this time has elapsed.

Critical Injuries do not use Recovery [[Success Targets and Hits#Hits|Hits]]. Instead, recovery can be accelerated through rest and medical care.

Once per week, if the character has spent at least 4 days resting, receiving care, or performing only light activity, make a Vigor [[Skills#Attribute Checks|Attribute Check]].

For each positive Hit, reduce the remaining Recovery time by 1 additional day, to a maximum of 7 days reduced by a single weekly roll.

A failed test provides no additional recovery, but time still passes normally.

If the character has multiple active Critical Injuries, make only one recovery test per week. Any days removed by the test may be divided between those injuries as the player chooses.

A [[Complications|Complication]] adds 1d6 days to the remaining Recovery time of one active Critical Injury after applying any days removed by the successful test.

Strenuous activity, further injury, poor living conditions, or lack of necessary care may prevent the weekly recovery test at the GM's discretion.
