
Skills are what a character has learned through training, practice and experience. [[Attributes]] describe what comes naturally. Skills describe what the character can do reliably because they've done it before.

Skills are rated from 0 to 60. A test adds the Skill's rating to its linked Attribute to get the [[Success Targets and Hits|Success Target]].

Each Skill lists the Attribute it normally uses. That's the usual approach, not a restriction. If the character is going about it differently, the player can suggest another Attribute and the GM decides. Athletics normally uses Strength, but vaulting across rooftops might use Agility instead.

Skills come in two kinds: **Core Skills** and **Expertise Skills**.

## Core Skills

Core Skills are broad abilities anyone can attempt without special training. A character can use a Core Skill even with a rating of 0.

**Athletics (Strength):** Climbing, swimming, jumping, sprinting, lifting and other physical exertion.

**Con (Instinct):** Lying, bluffing, misdirection, and creating or keeping up a false impression.

**Dodge (Agility):** Evading melee attacks, hazards and other sudden physical dangers. Dodge can't be used against ranged attacks; see [[Active and Bonus Defense]].

**Driving (Instinct):** Operating ordinary ground [[Operating Vehicles|vehicles]] such as cars, motorcycles, vans and trucks. Vehicles that need specialized training use an Expertise Skill instead.

**First Aid (Logic):** Stabilizing injuries, stopping bleeding and giving basic emergency care. Proper diagnosis and treatment need Medicine.

**Influence (Persona):** Persuading, negotiating and bargaining, convincing people through reason, appeal or compromise.

**Intimidation (Resolve):** Applying pressure through threats, fear or force of will. Physical menace may use Strength instead.

**Investigation (Logic):** Searching for clues, examining evidence, piecing events together, and digging up information through ordinary digital sources.

**Leadership (Persona):** Giving orders, keeping morale up, and coordinating people under pressure.

**Marksmanship (Agility):** Using firearms and other ranged weapons that rely on aim and controlled handling.

**Melee (Agility):** Fighting up close, armed or unarmed.

**Navigation (Logic):** Finding routes, reading maps and keeping your bearings in unfamiliar places.

**Stealth (Agility):** Moving unseen, hiding and avoiding detection.

**Survival (Instinct):** Enduring hostile environments, finding shelter and resources, tracking, and dealing with wilderness hazards.

**Wit (Instinct):** Provoking, distracting, baiting or verbally wrong-footing someone. Wit changes how someone reacts. It doesn't convince them something is true.

## Expertise Skills

Expertise Skills cover specialized training, professional knowledge and technical disciplines. A character needs a rating of at least 1 to use one. Without the right Expertise, a task may be impossible, or the character may have to find another, less effective way to do it.

**Acting (Persona):** Playing a role, keeping up false mannerisms, and performing convincingly.

**Animal Handling (Persona):** Training, directing, calming and caring for animals.

**Armorer (Agility):** Building, repairing, maintaining and modifying weapons and armor.

**Bioware (Logic):** Installing, maintaining, diagnosing and modifying biological augmentations.

**Boating (Instinct):** Operating watercraft, from surface vessels to submersibles.

**Bureaucracy (Logic):** Dealing with procedures, permits, records, corporate administration and formal organizations.

**Chemistry (Logic):** Identifying, analyzing and synthesizing chemical substances.

**Coding (Logic):** Writing, modifying, debugging and analyzing software.

**Cyberware (Logic):** Installing, maintaining, diagnosing and modifying cybernetic augmentations.

**Demolitions (Logic):** Preparing, placing, disarming and safely handling explosives and breaching charges.

**Disguise (Agility):** Creating physical disguises that change or hide someone's identity. Selling the disguise in person uses Persona instead.

**Electronics (Logic):** Diagnosing, repairing, modifying and building electronic devices, circuits, sensors and communications gear.

**Engineering (Logic):** Designing, analyzing and modifying complex technological or structural systems.

**Forensics (Logic):** Examining physical evidence and pulling technical information from scenes, remains, materials and traces.

**Forgery (Logic or Instinct):** Creating convincing fake documents, credentials, signatures, records and physical copies.

**Hacking (Instinct):** Breaking into, manipulating, defending and compromising digital systems and networks.

**Mechanics (Logic or Instinct):** Repairing, maintaining, modifying and diagnosing engines, machinery and mechanical systems.

**Medicine (Logic):** Diagnosing illness and injury, giving advanced treatment, prescribing drugs and managing long-term care.

**Performance (Persona):** Singing, acting on stage, public speaking and other performances meant to move an audience. Dance and musical instruments may use Agility instead.

**Piloting (Instinct):** Flying aircraft, VTOLs and other [[Operating Vehicles|vehicles]] that need specialized flight training.

**Ritual Magic (Anima):** Performing [[Ritual Magic|rituals]] through preparation, time and careful procedure.

**Spellcasting (Anima):** Shaping and releasing [[Spellcasting|spells]].

**Summoning (Anima):** Contacting, manifesting, commanding, binding and banishing [[Summoning|spirits]].

**Tactics (Logic):** Reading combat situations, planning operations, anticipating the enemy and coordinating a tactical approach.

**Thievery (Agility):** Picking locks, sleight of hand, pickpocketing and getting past mundane physical security.

This list isn't complete. Other professions, technical fields and arts can be added as Expertise Skills where they fit.

## Languages

Languages are recorded separately from Skills. Each language a character knows has a **Proficiency** that covers speaking, understanding, reading and writing it. Proficiency isn't rolled. It decides what kind of communication is possible and whether a test takes a penalty.

| Proficiency | Capability | Effect on tests |
|---|---|---|
| Rudimentary | Common words, signs, numbers, greetings and a few memorized phrases. No real conversation. | Only very simple communication. Anything needing real conversation, detailed reading or precise expression usually can't be attempted. |
| Basic | Simple needs and straightforward statements about familiar subjects. Fast or complex conversation is hard. | −20 on tests that depend heavily on language. |
| Conversational | Ordinary conversation, normal speech and writing. Nuance and specialized vocabulary can still cause trouble. | −10 on tests that rely on nuance, precise wording, wordplay or subtle interpretation. |
| Fluent | Comfortable in almost any situation, including complex conversation. | No penalty. |
| Native | Full command of idiom, slang, dialect and cultural references. | No penalty. No bonus either, but full familiarity with slang, accents and other cues. |

When language matters, use the normal Skill or Attribute Check. Knowing a language doesn't give specialized knowledge: legal terms, medical vocabulary, magical notation or technical documents may still need an Expertise Skill or a Memory check.

## Attribute Checks

When no Skill fits, make an **Attribute Check**: add the two Attributes the character is relying on. If one Attribute clearly covers the whole action, add it to itself.

These checks come up often:

**Perception (Instinct + Resolve):** Noticing details, spotting movement, hearing unusual sounds, and staying aware of the surroundings.

**Insight (Instinct + Logic):** Reading intentions, spotting lies, and understanding what someone is really after.

**Composure (Resolve + Persona):** Keeping emotions in check, hiding reactions, and staying calm under social or psychological pressure.

**Memory (Logic + Resolve):** Recalling academic, professional, technical or formally learned information.

**Streetwise (Instinct + Resolve):** Recalling rumors, local knowledge, criminal know-how, routes and other things learned informally.

**Vigor (Fortitude + Resolve):** Resisting exhaustion, pain, illness and physical strain.

**Willpower (Resolve + Instinct):** Resisting fear, coercion, mental intrusion and anything else trying to override the character's will.

**Grip (Strength + Resolve):** Holding onto an object or a person, and resisting disarms or being forced to move.

The GM can call for other pairs when an action doesn't fit one of these. The Attributes should match what the character is actually relying on.

## Knowledge

Characters don't need to write down every subject they know. Most knowledge comes from their background, profession and experience, and from what makes sense in the story. Common knowledge doesn't need a test.

When it's unclear whether a character would know something, the GM can call for a check. Use **Memory** for things learned through school, training, research or study. Use **Streetwise** for things picked up through experience, rumor, subcultures, criminal circles and everyday life. The GM sets the difficulty by how obscure or specific the information is.

Knowing a subject exists isn't the same as understanding it professionally. Detailed medical, technical, magical, legal or scientific knowledge may need an Expertise Skill instead.
